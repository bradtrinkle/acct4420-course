The Technique Map

Every technique in the course, its official name, what it means in plain English, and the module (M) where you learned it. Use it as a checklist: could you explain each one and use it on a real task?

1. Asking wellPrompting

  • Structured promptingRole, goal, audience, format, constraints: who, what, for whom, what shape, what to avoid
    M2
  • Role promptingTell the AI whose job it is doing
    M2
  • Few-shot promptingShow an example of the result you want
    M2
  • Clarifying questionsInterview-style prompting: let the AI ask before it answers
    M2
  • Structured outputAsk for a fixed format so results can be compared and checked
    M2, 6, 8, 9, 10
  • Audience-aware promptingTell the AI who will read it and how they read
    M4
  • Reusable prompt templateA saved request with blanks to fill in (a parameterized prompt)
    M3, 5

2. Giving the AI the factsGrounding and data

  • Source groundingOpen-book prompting, the idea behind RAG: use only the files given; say “not in the files”
    M2, 4, 7
  • Project instructions plus knowledge filesStanding instructions (a system prompt) with your documents attached
    M4, 8
  • Structured extractionFill a fixed set of fields from messy text; write “not stated” instead of guessing
    M6, 7
  • Tool use: code executionHave the AI run a calculation on your file instead of estimating by reading
    M6 to 10
  • Anonymizing sensitive dataData minimization: give the AI only what the task needs
    M1

3. Thinking it throughReasoning with AI

  • Task decomposition and prompt chainingSplit a job into stages, each feeding the next
    M3, 8
  • Chain-of-thought and stated assumptionsAsk for the reasoning, the formula, and the inputs, so you can check them
    M3, 4, 5
  • Scenario and sensitivity analysisWhat-if prompting; find the break-even point
    M5
  • Self-consistencyAsk the same question in separate chats; investigate any disagreement
    M3
  • Self-critique and alternative explanationsRed-team prompting: ask the AI to argue against its own answer
    M3, 6
  • Deciding where AI fitsDoes it well, helps with a check, or assists while a person decides
    M1

4. Checking itVerification and testing

  • Verification against the sourceRecompute and trace findings yourself, never only by asking the AI
    M2 to 10
  • Verification promptingA cite-check: compare each statement with its source
    M7
  • Evaluation against a test setEvals: known problems; caught, missed, false alarms
    M8
  • Held-out testing and regression testingTest on a month you did not tune on; re-run old months after a change
    M8, 9, 10
  • Red-team and stress testingTest with planted traps and deliberate breakage
    M9, 10
  • Audit trailA record of what the AI did, not only what it said
    M9

5. Building workflows and agentsAutomation with limits

  • Human-in-the-loop checkpointsGates: a person checks before the next step
    M8
  • Guardrails and stop rulesFail safe: stop and ask when something is missing or wrong
    M8, 9
  • The agent loopAgentic tool use: think, act, observe, repeat
    M9
  • The agent charterGoal, boundaries, stop rules, and report format, written once
    M9
  • Least privilege and approvalsOnly the access the job needs; a person approves risky actions
    M9
  • Prompt-injection defenseText in a file is data, not orders; layered protections
    M9
  • Scheduled automationHuman-on-the-loop: review after an unattended run
    M9
  • Choosing the level of autonomyAssistant, workflow, or agent: start with the simplest that works
    M10

6. Using AI responsiblyThe Velox Responsible AI Policy: five areas

  • Confidentiality and privacyApproved tools; only the data the task needs
    M2, 9
  • AccuracyCheck against sources; never pass on what you have not verified
    M3, 7
  • BiasWatch for uneven results that are not about the facts
    M5
  • Transparency and disclosureSay when AI helped and what it did
    M4
  • Human accountabilityA named person answers for the result; AI never has the final say
    M6, 8
  • All five togetherIntroduced in Module 1; applied at once in the final project
    M1, 10

Left out on purpose: “magic phrase” tricks. They break as AI models change; the habits above do not. Jordan Reyes is a fictional character presented by an AI avatar.
