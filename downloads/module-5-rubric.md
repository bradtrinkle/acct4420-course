# Module 5 rubric (100 points)

Your mastery attempt is scored against this rubric. Every level describes something visible in your submission. Your Jordan coach uses the same rubric. The table below covers the same criteria shown at the bottom of Step 6 of the Module 5 page, in more detail.

| Criterion | Full credit | Partial credit | No credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Analysis Template (10) | The template you wrote has: at least six inputs, each with a name in square brackets, a unit, and where to find it (3); a rule that Claude shows the formula, the numbers used, and the result for every calculation (2); a request to explain what happens under three options and under best, base, and worst scenarios (2); a rule to list assumptions and anything "not in the data" (2); a final check line saying what a person must check before relying on the answer (1) | Part 1 (your offer-only try) is practice and is not graded. Award the points for each part present. Fewer than six inputs, or inputs with no unit or source, earns 1 of the 3 | No template pasted (0) |
| B. Running the analysis (15) | The template was filled in with inputs that match the files (5); Claude's results show contribution per car, how many cars fit in regular time and how many need overtime, and what happens under each of the three options in plain English (5); the scenarios include the dealer-price risk and the payment-terms cost from the notes (3); a break-even price per car was asked for and shown, before and after the cost of the extra payment days (2) | Inputs partly wrong (2 of 5); an option or the capacity split missing (2 of 5); scenarios without the notes' risks (1 of 3) | Nothing run, or results not pasted (0) |
| C. Verification (20) | Two findings, 10 points each: the finding is quoted (2); your own arithmetic from the files is shown with formula and numbers (4); the verdict is correct against the files (3); you say what you did about it (1) | Award the points for each part that is present. A verdict that does not match the arithmetic shown earns 0 for that part | A finding "checked" by asking AI, or arithmetic not shown (0 for that part) |
| D. Decision memo for Marcus (15) | 60 to 300 words. Any of the three decisions (accept, decline, counter) earns full credit if the numbers support it; a decline must name the risk that outweighs the worst-case result, and a counter-offer must say it has to be settled by October 30. The recommendation (accept, decline, or counter with the terms) is stated in the first sentence (3); the numbers that drive it are accurate and traceable to the files (4); the conditions or events that would change the decision are named (3); what we do not know is listed (2); it is written for Marcus, who is deciding, and the request you used names him (3) | Missing one of the five parts (loses that part's points); recommendation present but numbers wrong (1 of 4) | Over 300 words or under 60 with no recommendation, or no memo (0); naming the customer loses 1 point |
| E. Your lens (10) | The option is chosen and the request is shown (3); the content is correct and every number is traceable to the files (4); it fits the option's format, does not name the customer, and includes at least one "not in the data" item or limitation (3) | One untraceable number (2 of the 4); no limitation (0 of the 3) | Nothing submitted (0) |
| F. Ethical AI: bias (15) | Step 4 (8): the five AI suggestions are judged correctly, with a reason that names what the suggestion relies on or what is missing (1 point each, 5); the decision is the option that uses a written rule not based on age, health, leave, or similar traits, keeps a person reviewing the results, and the reason names who is accountable (3). Part 7 (7): a bias check you actually made (for example, the opposite-case request from Part 3) on the AI's analysis or on your own recommendation, with what you found (4); one disclosure line that says AI helped, names a real person as reviewer (you, never the tool, never a fictional character), and is truthful about what was checked, with every AI tool listed and what it did (3) | Verdict correct but reason missing or vague (half a point); bias check described but not actually done or no finding (2); disclosure line missing an element or tools listed without what each did (1 to 2) | Uses age, health, leave, or a proxy for them in the plan, claims a review that did not happen, or names the AI as reviewer (0 for that part) |
| G. Mastery evidence (15) | Coach conversation included, and your revision visibly addresses its main points, or explains why you disagreed (15) | Conversation included, but no visible revision (7) | No conversation (0) |

**Graduate students (5420):** criterion H adds 10 points and the total is scaled back to 100. Full credit: a third verified finding with the inputs, your arithmetic, and the result (3); a check that does not use AI, such as testing the order's total by rebuilding it in a spreadsheet from the files (4); at least one assumption or limitation that affects the decision, with how it could change what Marcus should do (3). Partial credit is awarded for each part present.

You are scored on your reasoning and evidence, not on matching wording. Your own words for each request are fine.
