Step 3 · Learn · 75 min

3a. What generative AI is

A large language model (LLM) predicts likely next words from patterns in huge amounts of text. That is why it writes fluently and explains concepts well, and why it can sound confident while being wrong. Claude is a large language model, and so are ChatGPT and Gemini.

Your turn

Your manager says, "AI looked it up, so it's correct." Is that a safe assumption? (Click an answer to see right away whether you were correct. Practice questions are not graded.)

3b. Where AI is strong

Drafting, summarizing, explaining, translating, organizing, brainstorming, and a first pass at data you give it. In finance, that is hours back every week.

3c. Where AI needs checking

Large calculations, current facts like tax rates, citations, and anything it cannot see, such as Velox's own records. When AI fills a gap with something made up, that is called a hallucination. "Large" calculations means multi-step or high-volume math: AI can slip, so recompute it. Always check a fact against the original source, the agency or document that publishes it.

Your turn

Suppose you ask AI for the current Iowa corporate income tax rate. Where would you check its answer?

3d. The three-way sort

Every task gets AI help. The question is how much you check and who owns the decision. A rule of thumb: if a mistake would cost money or reach people outside Velox, a person at least checks. If the task needs judgment or a signature, a person decides. There is often more than one defensible answer, so your reasoning matters most.

CategoryMeaningExample
AI does it wellLow risk; a quick read is enoughDrafting a routine email
AI helps, a person checksAI does the work; you verify facts and numbersSummarizing an internal policy memo for a coworker
AI assists, a person decidesAI prepares the analysis; a named person makes the judgment or signsRecommending a receivable write-off
